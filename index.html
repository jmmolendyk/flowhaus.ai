The opportunity

AI can change how your organization works.

The value is real: faster work, better decisions, less repetitive effort. It shows up when AI is tied to a clear strategy, fitted into the processes people already use, and adopted by the people doing the work.

The reality

AI is arriving faster than organizations can absorb it.

  • Strategy without follow-through

    AI goals get set, but they don't reach the processes and teams that would carry them out.

  • Tools without adoption

    Pilots launch and licenses get bought, but people's work doesn't change, or they reach for their own tools instead.

  • Use without visibility

    Teams already use AI tools and agents that nobody inventoried or approved.

  • Rules that don't fit

    Policy stays on paper while ISO/IEC 42001, the NIST AI RMF, and the EU AI Act move faster than internal readiness. Heavy-handed governance gets worked around.

Approach

Optimize, then automate.

A principle from my Six Sigma work, applied to AI adoption: fix the process first, then add technology, with controls proportionate to the risk.

  1. Define the problem and the measure

    Identify the opportunity, its business value, and how success will be measured.

  2. Optimize, then automate

    Redesign the process, then build the solution around the new needs and the technology available, AI included, with responsibility built in from the start.

  3. Measure and improve

    Track performance against the original measures and make changes as you learn.

  4. Hand over ownership

    Transfer the solution to its owner with documentation, training, and performance visibility.

Governance as an enabler

Lightweight, proportionate controls help teams adopt AI faster, because they know where the boundaries are.

Design for adoption

Human-centered design makes expectations clear through readable disclosures, intuitive workflows, and controls suited to the context.
