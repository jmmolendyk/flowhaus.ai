ISO/IEC 42001

ISO/IEC 42001 implementation, built on what you already have.

ISO/IEC 42001 is the international standard for AI management systems. As a BSI-certified Lead Implementer, I help organizations build the management system, start from the ISO/IEC 27001 work already in place, and prepare for the certification audit.

I'm not a certification body and can't certify you. An accredited body does that independently, which is what gives the certificate its value.

Starting points

Where are you starting?

Two things shape the path: whether you already run an ISO management system, and how AI is governed today. Most organizations fall into one of three starting points.

  • Starting point 1

    You hold ISO/IEC 27001

    You already operate a management system with context, leadership, risk, internal audit, and management review in place.

    The work: extend it. Map what carries over, add the AI-specific requirements, and integrate the audits where your certification body allows.

  • Starting point 2

    You have some governance, or none

    AI is in use, with informal guidance or documented processes for some activities, but no management system tying it together.

    The work: build a proportionate AI management system: scope, inventory, risk and impact assessment, policy, and the controls that apply to you.

  • Starting point 3

    You are preparing for the audit

    The system exists and you want to know whether it will stand up to an independent assessment.

    The work: audit readiness. Internal audit, management review, evidence, and a walkthrough of Stage 1 and Stage 2 before the certification body arrives.

From ISO/IEC 27001

ISO/IEC 27001 is a strong starting point.

ISO/IEC 42001 and ISO/IEC 27001 follow the same harmonized structure, with clauses 4 to 10 in the same order. If you already run an information security management system, much of the management-system work exists. What remains is the AI-specific layer.

ClauseAlready in place with ISO/IEC 27001What AI adds
4 ContextOrganizational context, interested parties, and the scope of the management system.Your role in relation to AI systems, AI-specific stakeholder expectations, and a scope that covers your AI systems.
5 LeadershipTop-management commitment, policy, and assigned roles.An AI policy and clear accountability for AI.
6 PlanningRisk assessment and treatment, objectives, and a Statement of Applicability.AI risk assessment, AI system impact assessment, and a Statement of Applicability against the 38 Annex A controls.
7 SupportResources, competence, awareness, communication, and documented information.AI literacy and competence, and documentation specific to AI systems.
8 OperationOperational planning and control.The AI system life cycle, data used for AI, and third-party and supplier relationships.
9 Performance evaluationMonitoring, internal audit, and management review.AI performance monitoring, with the existing audit and review programs extended to the AI management system.
10 ImprovementNonconformity, corrective action, and continual improvement.The same process, applied to AI incidents and failures.

Not a prerequisite

ISO/IEC 27001 certification is not required for ISO/IEC 42001. It simply gives you a head start, because the management-system machinery already runs.

Fewer duplicated efforts

Shared policies, procedures, and records can serve both standards. Where your certification body offers it, the two can be audited together.

What 27001 does not cover

ISO/IEC 27001 protects information. ISO/IEC 42001 adds AI-specific concerns such as impact on people, the data used for AI, transparency, and human oversight.

The path

From gap assessment to a working management system.

How long this takes depends on your scope, your size, and your starting point. An established ISO/IEC 27001 system starts you further along.

  1. 01

    Scope and gap assessment

    Define which AI systems and activities the management system covers, then compare current practice, including any existing ISMS, against ISO/IEC 42001.

  2. 02

    Inventory, risk, and impact

    Inventory the AI in scope, assess risk and system impact, and decide which Annex A controls apply. Record the decisions, and the reasons for any exclusions, in the Statement of Applicability.

  3. 03

    Build the management system

    Put in place the policy, roles, procedures, and controls, sized to your organization and reusing the processes your ISMS already runs.

  4. 04

    Operate and gather evidence

    Run the system long enough to produce the records an auditor will ask to see.

  5. 05

    Internal audit and management review

    Test the system yourselves first, and have leadership review it formally, before the certification audit.

  6. 06

    Certification audit and beyond

    An accredited certification body audits in two stages. The certificate is valid for three years and maintained through annual surveillance audits.

Audit readiness

Know what the auditor will ask.

The certification audit has two stages. Preparing for each one is mostly a matter of having the right evidence, organized and understood by the people who will be interviewed.

Stage 1: documentation and design

Is the management system designed to meet the standard, and is the organization ready for the full audit?

  • Scope and AI policy
  • AI risk and impact assessments
  • Statement of Applicability
  • Internal audit and management review records

Stage 2: operation and effectiveness

Does the system work in practice, across the people and AI systems in scope?

  • Interviews with leadership and system owners
  • Sampling of records and controls in operation
  • Incidents, corrective actions, and monitoring
  • Progress against AI objectives

Questions

Questions worth asking early.

Do we need ISO/IEC 27001 first?

No. ISO/IEC 27001 is not a prerequisite for ISO/IEC 42001. If you already hold it, you can extend the management system you have. If you don't, you can build an AI management system directly, sized to your organization.

Is certification the right goal for us?

It depends on who is asking for evidence. Customers, partners, and regulators increasingly want independent assurance for consequential AI. For other organizations, foundations come first, and rigor can be added where the consequence is higher. That is the idea behind Progressive AI Governance.

What does certification actually certify?

A management system, within a scope you define. It asks whether you have the policies, roles, risk processes, and controls to manage AI over time, and whether they work in practice. It is not a rating of any model or product.

How do we show that our AI performs and can be trusted?

By defining how you will evaluate it, verifying and monitoring it through its life cycle, and keeping the records. An auditor looks for evidence that you do this consistently, not for guarantees about any one system's accuracy.

Can the ISO/IEC 27001 and 42001 audits be combined?

Often, yes. Because the standards share a structure, many certification bodies offer combined or integrated audits. Ask early, since it affects how you plan the audit calendar.
