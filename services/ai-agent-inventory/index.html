AI & Agent Inventories

You can't govern what you can't see. An inventory is the list of every AI system, tool, and autonomous agent your organization uses or builds, who owns each one, what data it touches, and how much risk it carries.

Why it comes first

Most organizations use more AI than anyone has written down: tools a team signed up for, AI features switched on inside existing software, third-party APIs, and agents that act on someone's behalf. Policy, risk assessment, and readiness work all depend on knowing what is actually there.

20%of organizations studied reported a breach tied to shadow AI, meaning AI used without employer approval or oversight
$670Kadded to the average breach cost where shadow AI was widespread, compared with little or none
63%of breached organizations had no AI governance policy to manage AI or prevent shadow AI

Source: IBM, Cost of a Data Breach Report 2025 (Ponemon Institute, 600 organizations)

The frameworks point the same way. The NIST AI Risk Management Framework names it directly: mechanisms are in place to inventory AI systems, resourced according to organizational risk priorities (GOVERN 1.6). ISO/IEC 42001 doesn't have a control called "inventory," but several of its requirements are hard to evidence without one, and the EU AI Act's risk tiers can't be applied to systems you haven't identified.

What goes into it

  1. Scope and definitions.Agree what counts as an AI system for your organization, including embedded features, third-party APIs, and agents, so the inventory has clear edges.
  2. Discovery.Combine what people tell us (short interviews and a lightweight survey) with what the systems show (procurement, SSO and app lists, expense records, vendor contracts) to surface shadow AI.
  3. Capture.Record each system's purpose, owner, vendor, data it touches, who it affects, and where a human stays in the loop.
  4. Classify.Sort each entry by risk and regulatory exposure so attention goes where it matters first.
  5. Hand off and maintain.Set the ownership and review rhythm so the inventory stays accurate after the engagement ends.

What you have at the end

These are the kinds of artifacts this work produces. What you receive depends on your size, your risk, and how you already work.

  • AI registerOne working record of every system, with owner, purpose, data, and status.
  • Risk and exposure viewEach entry classified so high-risk and regulated uses are visible at a glance.
  • Gaps and first actionsA short list of what to fix first: unowned systems, missing approvals, unmanaged vendors.
  • Maintenance routineWho updates the register, when, and what triggers a review.
