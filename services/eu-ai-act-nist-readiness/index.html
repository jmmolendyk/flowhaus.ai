03 Prove it

EU AI Act & NIST AI RMF Readiness

Know where you stand against the EU AI Act and the NIST AI RMF: your risk tier, your documentation gaps, and what to do first.

Why it matters

The EU AI Act applies based on where AI is placed on the market or used, not just where a company is based. It sorts AI into risk tiers, and each tier carries different duties. The NIST AI RMF is voluntary, but it is the common reference point many US customers, regulators, and standards point to. A readiness review tells you which of these apply to you and what is missing.

DateWhat applies
2 Feb 2025Prohibited AI practices and AI literacy obligations apply
2 Aug 2025Obligations for general-purpose AI models apply
2 Aug 2026Most transparency obligations (Article 50) apply
2 Dec 2027High-risk obligations for stand-alone systems (Annex III) now apply, moved from August 2026
2 Aug 2028High-risk obligations for AI built into regulated products (Annex I) now apply

Source: K&L Gates on the EU Digital Omnibus on AI and Gibson Dunn

Dates reflect the Digital Omnibus on AI as understood in November 2026 and can change. I'm not a lawyer: this work shows you where you stand and what to do first, and where a legal opinion is needed I'll tell you and refer you to independent counsel.

What goes into it

  1. Establish applicability.Work out which laws and frameworks apply to you, as a provider, deployer, or both, and in which markets.
  2. Classify your systems.Place each system in the EU AI Act's risk tiers using your inventory, and note what the NIST AI RMF would expect.
  3. Assess the gaps.Compare current policies, documentation, oversight, and records against what each tier requires.
  4. Prioritize.Rank gaps by risk and by deadline so the first actions are clear.
  5. Plan the work.Produce a roadmap with owners and sequencing, sized to your resources.
