02 Govern it

Corporate AI Policies

Clear, usable rules: what data goes where, how outputs are vetted, and who owns what. A policy is only useful if people can follow it on a busy Tuesday.

Why it matters

People are already using AI at work, with or without a policy. The question is whether they're guided or guessing. Good policy is short, specific, and written for the people who have to use it, with the detail kept in standards and procedures underneath.

78%of AI users were bringing their own AI tools to work (80% at small and medium-sized businesses), per a 2024 survey
39%of AI users globally had received AI training from their company
63%of breached organizations had no AI governance policy to manage AI or prevent shadow AI (IBM, 2025)

Source: Microsoft and LinkedIn, 2024 Work Trend Index (31,000 knowledge workers); IBM, Cost of a Data Breach Report 2025

A policy also gives your other work something to stand on. Inventories, assessments, vendor reviews, and certification all ask whether rules exist and whether people follow them.

What goes into it

  1. Listen first.Learn how AI is really being used, what people need from it, and where the current rules, if any, get in the way.
  2. Set the principles.Agree a few clear commitments that leadership will stand behind.
  3. Draft in plain language.Write the policy around real decisions: acceptable use, data handling, human review, disclosure, vendors, and ownership.
  4. Test it.Walk real scenarios past the people who'll use it, and fix what confuses them.
  5. Roll out and set a review date.Plan communication and training, and set when the policy gets revisited.

What you have at the end

These are the kinds of artifacts this work produces. What you receive depends on your size, your risk, and how you already work.

  • AI policyA short, readable policy approved by the right people.
  • Quick-reference guideA one-page "what can I do with AI?" for everyday use.
  • Approval and exception pathHow to request a new tool or use case, and who decides.
  • Rollout planCommunication and training outline, with a review date.
