AI Risk & Impact Assessments

Find bias, privacy, and safety issues before a high-stakes system goes live, not after. An assessment looks at what a system does to the people it affects, and what that means for your organization.

Why it matters

Risk assessments ask what could go wrong for the organization. Impact assessments ask what the system does to the people and groups it acts on. Both are easier, and cheaper, to do before launch than to reconstruct after a complaint, an audit, or an incident.

2025ISO/IEC 42005 published, the first international guidance on how to run an AI system impact assessment
6.1.4the ISO/IEC 42001 clause that requires a process for AI system impact assessments
Dec 2027new application date for EU AI Act high-risk obligations on stand-alone systems, which include the fundamental rights impact assessment duty

Source: CMS on ISO/IEC 42005 and K&L Gates on the EU Digital Omnibus on AI

These are related but separate assessments. ISO/IEC 42005 is guidance, not a certifiable standard. A privacy impact assessment under GDPR and a fundamental rights impact assessment under the EU AI Act are legal duties with their own triggers. A good assessment is built once and connects to the others rather than duplicating them. For legal conclusions, I'll point you to counsel.

What goes into it

  1. Triage.Decide which systems need a full assessment and which need a lighter review, using your inventory and clear thresholds.
  2. Describe the system.Document purpose, intended and foreseeable uses, data, model, deployment context, and who is affected.
  3. Identify impacts and risks.Work through bias, privacy, safety, security, and misuse with the people who build, run, and are affected by the system.
  4. Rate and decide.Rate likelihood and severity, set controls, and record who accepted any remaining risk.
  5. Document and schedule review.Produce a record that stands up to an auditor, with triggers for reassessment when the system or its use changes.

What you have at the end
